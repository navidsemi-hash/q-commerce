04 · User Segmentation & Product–Market Fit

Not all "forgetful" users are equal — we segmented the 81%

01 Primary target · V1
High-frequency forgetters
21% of all users — they forget items multiple times a week.
  • Drive 65% of the duplicate-order volume
  • Highest immediate ROI for the 15-minute window
02 Secondary target
Low-frequency forgetters
60% of all users — they forget items once a month or less.
  • Lower volume, but highly vocal about the frustration
  • A simple UI captures this long tail without feature bloat
PM takeaway — by prioritising high-frequency forgetters, who drive the majority of duplicate orders, we solved the problem at its root: the highest immediate ROI from the 15-minute window.

06 · Solution Strategy & Stakeholder Alignment

A 15-minute, add-only editing window — and how we got everyone to yes

The concept
A 15-minute add-only window — matched to the 5–10 min prep window plus a payment buffer, closing before picking starts.
Why "add-only"
Adding items is operationally simple; removing means complex inventory reversal. Add-only solved 89% of user needs without breaking fulfilment workflows.
Navigating stakeholder friction
The PM asked for a 30-minute window. I pushed back with vendor data — 5–10 min prep; 30 min would cause operational chaos. We compromised on 15 minutes.
Getting vendor buy-in
Vendors were initially resistant. The pitch: automating the manual cash add-on saves them time and reduces errors — they actively supported the rollout once they saw the relief.

We prioritised failure modes by user impact and operational effort

High impact · low effortShipped V1
Item out of stock — UI shows "Unavailable" and disables the Add button.
Edit after 15 minutes — the button gracefully degrades to "Reorder".
High impact · high effortDeferred V2
Removing items — requires complex inventory reversal and refund logic.
Low impact · low effortShipped V1
Price changes — real-time pricing applied with a simple toast notification.
Payment fails — non-blocking flow; the original order proceeds uninterrupted.
Low impact · high effort
Nothing shipped here — not worth the engineering time.

PM takeaway — we invested engineering time only where it protected the core user journey and the vendor workflow.

09 · A/B Testing Results & the Trade-Offs

Four weeks, ~500,000 live orders — the wins, and the reality check

−38%Order cancellations — from forgotten items
−58%Support inquiries — net aggregate drop over 4 weeks
+22%Customer retention — 30-day repeat purchase rate
+15%Average order value — users add, not abandon
Reality check — the "add-only" constraint frustrated users who wanted to remove items, causing a temporary spike in "how do I remove" support queries in Week 1.
The fix — an in-context tooltip explaining why removals weren't available yet. That query normalised by Week 2, so the net −58% aggregate support drop held steady.

11 · Go-to-Market & Adoption Strategy

A feature isn't successful if no one uses it — here's how we drove adoption

01
Phased rollout & A/B testing
A 4-week, 50/50 split test on ~500,000 live orders — monitoring vendor capacity, ensuring system stability, and validating the metrics before full launch.
02
User education
In-app tooltips on the home page. Push notifications for active orders: "Forgot something? You have 12 mins left to add items!"
03
Vendor onboarding
A clear, high-contrast "Edit Window Open" badge in the vendor app, plus brief digital training on the new automated flow vs the old manual cash method.
04
Developer efficiency
Documented every component state in Figma, significantly reducing back-and-forth and accelerating the 8-week sprint.

Learnings · What's Next
PM philosophy
Constraints drive focus
The 5–10 min prep time forced a simple, high-impact solution. Perfect is the enemy of shipped.
Data wins arguments
Pushing back on the PM's 30-min request with vendor data saved the operational workflow.
Adoption > features
Building the edit window was 20% of the work; educating users and onboarding vendors was the other 80%.
