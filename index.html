Stakeholder Alignment

How I Changed the Business Team's Mind

01 Why they pushed back
Their initial thought
The business team rejected the idea for three reasons:
  • Extra Revenue. "Duplicate orders bring extra delivery fees."
  • Too Complex. "Editing will break our fast logistics."
  • Low Priority. "Not urgent enough to fix right now."
02 My argument & solution
A safe solution
I pushed back with evidence — support-ticket patterns, survey data and journey analysis — and a safe solution: a 15-minute, "add-only" window.
  • Wasted Slots. 1-item orders waste trips meant for big carts.
  • Angry Users. Paying twice frustrates users and erodes trust.
  • Safe for Logistics. Adding items is simple. The 15-minute limit fits the packing time and doesn't break our workflow.
The result — they accepted my offer. Because the "add-only" rule avoided complex refunds, we moved forward.

Solution Strategy

A 15-minute, add-only editing window

0 MINOrder placed
5 MINVendor prep begins
10 MINPayment settled
15 MINHard cutoff · picking starts
20 MINOut for delivery
Why 15 minutes?
Matches the 5–10 min vendor prep window plus a payment-processing buffer — a hard cutoff before the "picking started" trigger fires.
Why add-only?
Adding is operationally simple; removing means inventory reversal and repacking.

A/B Testing Results & the Trade-Offs

Four weeks, ~500,000 live orders — the wins, and the reality check

−38%Order cancellations — relative drop vs. control
−58%Support inquiries — net aggregate drop over 4 weeks
+22%Customer retention — 30-day repeat purchase rate
+15%Average order value — users add, not abandon
Reality check — the "add-only" constraint frustrated users who wanted to remove items, causing a temporary spike in "how do I remove" support queries in Week 1.
The fix — an in-context tooltip explaining why removals weren't available yet. That query normalised by Week 2, so the net −58% aggregate support drop held steady.
